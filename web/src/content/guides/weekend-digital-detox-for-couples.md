"Digital detox" can sound like a punishment: lock the phones in a box and grit your teeth until Monday. This guide suggests something friendlier. For one weekend, the two of you put away the parts of your phones that eat your time, keep the parts that are useful, and plan a couple of days you will actually enjoy.

The aim is not to prove anything. It is to find out what a weekend feels like when your attention has only one place to go.

## Decide what kind of weekend you want

There are two versions. Choose together.

**The gentle version (recommended).** Phones stay on for calls, messages from close family, maps and photos. Social media, news, email, video and games are paused from Friday evening to Sunday evening.

**The full version.** Phones are switched off or left at home, apart from one that stays on loud for emergencies.

The gentle version is easier to keep and closer to how you might want to live afterwards. Whichever you pick, it applies to both of you equally.

## Before the weekend: 30 minutes of preparation

A little planning on Thursday or Friday makes the difference between a restful weekend and two people pacing the living room.

1. **Tell the people who might worry.** A quick message: "We're mostly offline this weekend. Call if you need us."
2. **Finish what would nag at you.** Pay the bill, answer the email, check the train times.
3. **Plan the anchors.** Choose two or three things to look forward to: a hike, a market, a recipe, a film at the cinema, friends for lunch.
4. **Get what you need offline.** Directions, tickets, a recipe written on paper, a playlist downloaded, a book each.
5. **Set up the phones.** Pause the tempting apps for the weekend. On iPhone you can do this with Screen Time. See our guide to [pausing social media on iPhone](/guides/pause-social-media-at-night-iphone/), and set the schedule to cover the whole weekend.
6. **Agree on exceptions.** If one of you must check work once, decide when and for how long.
7. **Choose a home for the phones.** A shelf by the door. That is where they live until Sunday evening.

## A plan for the weekend

Use this as a starting point and change anything you like.

### Friday evening: begin

- **Phones to the shelf** when you are both home.
- **A proper dinner.** Cook together or order in and set the table.
- **Talk about the weekend.** What does each of you want from it? Rest, adventure, a tidy home, time outside?
- **An early night,** with the phones charging outside the bedroom.

### Saturday: out and about

- **Slow morning.** Coffee, breakfast at the table, no rush.
- **Something active outdoors.** A long walk, a hike, a bike ride, a swim. Bring a picnic.
- **An afternoon with no plan.** Nap, read, wander. Boredom is allowed. It usually turns into something.
- **Cook something more ambitious than usual.** Fresh pasta, a curry from scratch, a cake.
- **An evening game or a film you chose together.** One screen, shared, is a different thing from two screens apart.

### Sunday: slow and close

- **Breakfast in bed or a walk to a café.**
- **A small project together.** Sort a shelf, plant something, fix the thing that has been waiting.
- **See people.** Lunch with friends or family, phones still away.
- **A long conversation.** Take a question or two from our [conversation starters](/guides/conversation-starters-for-couples/).
- **Sunday evening: close the weekend** with the short review below, before the phones come back.

For more ideas to fill the time, see our list of [phone-free date ideas](/guides/phone-free-date-ideas/).

## What to expect

**The first evening feels itchy.** You will reach for a phone that is not there, several times. This passes, usually by Saturday lunchtime.

**Time stretches.** Many people are surprised by how long a day is.

**You will be a bit bored.** Good. Boredom is where "shall we go for a walk?" comes from.

**You may talk about things you have been putting off.** That is a feature of having nothing else to look at. Be gentle with each other.

**Something will come up.** A message you need to send, a thing to look up. Do it, briefly, and put the phone back. One exception is not the end of the weekend.

## The Sunday evening review

Before you pick up your phones again, sit down for ten minutes and ask each other:

1. What was the best moment of the weekend?
2. What did you miss, and what did you not miss at all?
3. What was hard?
4. What is one thing we would like to keep?

That last question is the most valuable part of the whole exercise.

## What to keep afterwards

Do not try to keep everything. Pick one or two small things:

- **Phone-free dinners.**
- **Phones out of the bedroom.**
- **One screen-light day or half day each weekend.**
- **Protected evening hours on weekdays,** with social apps paused.
- **A monthly offline weekend.**

Write down what you chose, and agree when you will check in on it. Our guide to a [screen time agreement for couples](/guides/screen-time-agreement-for-couples/) has a template, and the [evening routine for couples](/guides/evening-routine-for-couples/) shows what weekday evenings can look like.

## If the weekend does not go to plan

Maybe work intruded, or one of you slipped back into scrolling on Saturday night. That is fine. Nothing was lost. You still learned something about what pulls you back and what you would like instead. Try again in a month, with what you learned.

And if one of you is keen and the other is not, do not push. Suggest a single phone-free afternoon instead. A detox only works when it is chosen by both.
