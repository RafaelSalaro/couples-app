For most of us the day begins the same way. The alarm goes off, on the phone. A hand reaches out to stop it, and since the phone is already in that hand, the thumb opens something. Messages, news, the weather, a feed. Ten minutes later you get up, already a little behind and already somewhere else.

Your partner, on the other side of the bed, is doing exactly the same thing.

A morning routine for two is a way to give the first minutes of the day to each other before the rest of the world gets a turn. It is short, it needs no special energy, and it changes the feel of the whole day.

## Why mornings are worth protecting

**The first thing you attend to sets your direction.** Start with a screen and the day starts with other people's news and requests. Start with your partner and it starts with your own life.

**Mornings are reliable.** Evenings get taken over by late meetings and tiredness. The first ten minutes after waking happen every single day.

**It is a small moment that works like a deposit.** A good morning hug and a proper goodbye take less than a minute, and they carry a surprising amount of warmth into the hours you spend apart.

## The routine: five small steps

### 1. Wake without the phone

This is the foundation, and it is decided the night before. Charge both phones outside the bedroom and wake up to an alarm clock. If the phone is not within reach, the morning scroll cannot begin.

If you need the phone as an alarm, put it across the room or just outside the door, so that you have to get up to turn it off, and leave it there.

Our guide to [keeping phones out of the bedroom](/guides/phones-out-of-the-bedroom/) covers the details.

### 2. A real good morning (1 minute)

Before either of you gets up, turn toward each other. Say good morning. Stay close for a minute. If one of you is already up, come back and say it.

It sounds almost too simple to write down. But many couples realize they have not done it in months.

### 3. A shared drink or breakfast (10 minutes)

Make the coffee or tea and drink it together, sitting down, at a table or on the steps or back in bed. Phones stay where they are.

You do not have to talk much. Morning quiet shared with someone is its own kind of closeness. If you do feel like talking, keep it light: how you slept, a dream, what the weather is doing.

Take turns making the drinks. Being brought a coffee in the morning is one of the small luxuries of living with someone.

### 4. One thing about the day ahead (2 minutes)

Each of you says one thing about your day: something you are looking forward to, something you are nervous about, or what time you will be home.

This matters more than it seems. It means that in the evening you can ask a real question, "How did the meeting go?", instead of a general "How was your day?"

### 5. A proper goodbye (30 seconds)

When the first person leaves, stop what you are doing. A hug, a kiss, a "good luck with the presentation". Then go.

If you both work from home, mark the start of the working day in the same way before you go to your separate desks.

## When do the phones come back?

Choose a moment and make it the same every day:

- after breakfast,
- when the first person leaves the house, or
- at a set time, such as 8 AM.

If you would like help holding to it, your phone can do that for you. On iPhone, a Screen Time schedule can keep social apps paused until the time you choose. The steps in our guide to [pausing social media at night on iPhone](/guides/pause-social-media-at-night-iphone/) work for mornings too: simply let the schedule run until after breakfast.

Checking something you need, such as a train time or a message about school, is fine. The habit is about not starting the day in a feed.

## The 5-minute version

For mornings when everything is late:

1. Phones stay outside the bedroom.
2. A real good morning.
3. One sentence each about the day.
4. A proper goodbye.

That is the whole thing, and it still counts.

## Different mornings, different versions

**One early bird, one night owl.** The early riser brings the other a drink and sits on the edge of the bed for two minutes before leaving. Or the shared moment is the goodbye. A note left by the kettle is a lovely substitute on very early days.

**Shift work.** Move the routine to whenever your "morning" is, or swap it for an evening version. See our [evening routine for couples](/guides/evening-routine-for-couples/).

**Children.** Mornings with children are loud and fast. Keep the three smallest pieces: good morning before the rush, a hug in the kitchen, and a proper goodbye. On weekends, try a slow breakfast for the whole family with all the phones away.

**Weekends.** This is where the routine can stretch. A long breakfast, the newspaper or a book, a walk before the day begins. Many couples say a slow, phone-free Saturday morning is their favorite hour of the week.

**Not morning people.** No conversation required. Sit next to each other, hold a warm mug, and wake up slowly. That is enough.

## Making it stick

**Start with step 1 only.** Getting the phones out of the bedroom makes every other step easy. Do that for a week before adding anything.

**Decide together.** Agree on which steps you both want. A routine only one of you chose will not last.

**Keep it the same for both of you.** If the phones wait until after breakfast, both phones wait.

**Expect missed mornings.** There will be alarms that fail and days that start in a hurry. Pick it up again the next day. Nobody needs to feel bad.

**Say when it is nice.** "I like our mornings" is worth saying out loud.
