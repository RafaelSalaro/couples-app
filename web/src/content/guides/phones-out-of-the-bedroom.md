Picture the end of an ordinary day. Two people in one bed, each lit by a small screen, each a little further away than the width of the mattress suggests. Eventually one phone goes down, then the other. "Night." "Night."

It does not have to end like that. Keeping phones out of the bedroom is one of the simplest changes a couple can make, and one of the ones people say they would never undo. This guide covers how to do it without drama.

## Why the bedroom matters

**It is the quietest time you have.** No tasks, no table to clear, nowhere to be. The minutes before sleep are when the small, unplanned conversations happen: the thing you forgot to mention, the worry you did not want to raise at dinner, the joke that only works in the dark.

**It sets the tone at both ends of the day.** What you do last at night and first in the morning colors everything in between. Ending the day turned toward each other feels different from ending it turned toward a feed.

**Phones in bed tend to take more than we plan.** "Five minutes" before sleep has a way of becoming forty.

**Sleep may thank you.** Many people find it easier to wind down without a bright, busy screen in the last half hour, and easier to stay asleep without a phone lighting up on the bedside table. We are not doctors, and this guide makes no medical promises. Try it for two weeks and judge for yourself.

## Step 1: Decide together

As with every shared habit, start with a conversation and not with a rule.

"I've been thinking it would be nice to keep our phones out of the bedroom. Want to try it for two weeks?"

Talk about what each of you uses the phone for at night. Alarm? Podcast to fall asleep? Checking tomorrow's weather? Messages from family in another time zone? Each of these has a simple answer, and naming them up front takes away the objections before they come up.

Agree that it applies to both of you. If one person is phone-free and the other is scrolling in bed, it will not last a week.

## Step 2: Build a charging spot outside the room

Choose a place that is not the bedroom: the hallway, the kitchen counter, a shelf in the living room. Put both chargers there. This is now where the phones sleep.

A few details that help:

- **Close, but outside.** Just beyond the bedroom door is fine, and you will still hear a call.
- **Both phones, same place.** It becomes a shared end-of-day ritual.
- **Sound on for calls, off for everything else.**

## Step 3: Replace what the phone was doing

The phone is in the bedroom because it does several jobs. Give each job to something else.

| The phone was your… | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Alarm | A basic alarm clock |
| Clock in the night | The same alarm clock |
| Bedtime reading | A book, a magazine or an e-reader |
| Podcast or music to fall asleep | A small speaker with a sleep timer, started before the phone leaves the room |
| White noise | A fan or a white noise machine |
| Torch | A small bedside lamp |
| Way to be reached | Phone outside the door, ringer on, calls from chosen people allowed |

The alarm clock is the one that matters. It is the most common reason people give for keeping the phone by the bed, and it costs very little to solve.

## Step 4: Set a "phones to bed" time

Pick a time, or a moment, when the phones go to their charging spot. Thirty minutes before you want to be asleep is a good start. Some couples tie it to a moment rather than the clock: when the kitchen is tidy, or when the first person goes to brush their teeth.

On iPhone, you can let the phone help. A sleep schedule with Sleep Focus quiets notifications at the time you choose, and Screen Time's Downtime can pause social apps for the night. Our guide explains how to [pause social media at night on iPhone](/guides/pause-social-media-at-night-iphone/).

## Step 5: Fill the last half hour with something good

Taking the phone away leaves a gap. Fill it on purpose, at least for the first couple of weeks.

- **Talk in the dark.** Ask, "What was the best part of today?"
- **Share one appreciation.** One thing the other person did today that you noticed.
- **Read.** Separately, side by side, or aloud to each other.
- **Stretch for five minutes.**
- **Plan something small for tomorrow** that you are both looking forward to.
- **Just be close.**

None of this needs to be elaborate. The point is only that the last thing you pay attention to each day is each other.

## Step 6: Give the morning the same care

The other half of this habit is the first ten minutes of the day. With the phone outside the room, you wake up to an alarm clock and a person rather than a list of notifications.

Try saying good morning, properly, before either of you goes to get a phone. It takes thirty seconds.

## The exceptions

**On-call work or family worries.** Keep the phone outside the door with the ringer on and allow calls from the right people. You will hear it.

**A new baby, or caring for someone.** Do whatever makes the nights easier. This habit will still be here later.

**Different bedtimes.** The earlier sleeper gets ten phone-free minutes of company at lights out. The night owl can go back to the living room afterwards, and the phone stays there when they come to bed.

**Travel.** Hotel rooms are small. Put the phones on the desk across the room, or in the bathroom.

**One of you is not convinced.** Go first with your own phone and do not make a point of it. After a week, say what you have noticed. An invitation works better than an argument.

## If you slip

You will bring the phone to bed some nights. Everyone does. It does not mean the habit is over, and nobody needs to be told off. Carry it back to the charger, or simply start again tomorrow night.
