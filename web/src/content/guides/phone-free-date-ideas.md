You sit down next to each other after a long day. One of you picks up a phone "just for a second". The other one does the same, because why not. Forty minutes later you are still side by side, and you have not said a word.

Nobody chose that evening. It just happened, the way it happens in most homes most nights. A phone-free date is simply an evening you choose on purpose. It does not need to be fancy or expensive. It needs two people and nothing buzzing between them.

Below are 45 ideas, sorted by how much time and energy you have. Pick one for tonight.

## First, make the phone-free part easy

The ideas are the fun part. The part that makes them work is small and practical.

- **Put the phones somewhere, not just down.** A phone face down on the table is still on the table. A drawer, a shelf by the door or a bag in another room works much better.
- **Decide the length before you start.** "Until the dishes are done" or "until nine" is easier to keep than an open-ended promise.
- **Handle the real worries first.** If someone might need to reach you, leave one phone on loud for calls. Then let it go.
- **Do it together.** This only feels good when it is mutual. If one of you is phone-free and the other is scrolling, it is not a date. It is waiting.
- **Skip the guilt.** If one of you reaches for a phone out of habit, laugh, put it back and carry on. This is practice, not a test.

## When you have 20 minutes

Short counts. A small pocket of full attention every day does more than a grand gesture once a season.

1. **Walk around the neighborhood.** No destination. Notice three things you have never seen before.
2. **Coffee or tea on the balcony, the steps or by a window.** Sit facing each other instead of side by side.
3. **Rose, thorn and bud.** Each share the best part of your day, the hard part, and something you are looking forward to.
4. **Stretch together.** Ten slow minutes on the floor. No routine needed.
5. **Dance to three songs in the kitchen.** One pick each, and one you both love.
6. **Play a quick card game.** Keep a deck where the remote usually lives.
7. **Read to each other.** A poem, a page of a novel, the funniest paragraph from your book.
8. **Cook the simplest thing together.** Eggs on toast counts if you make it side by side.
9. **Plan the weekend on paper.** One thing each of you wants, one thing for the two of you.
10. **Look at the sky.** Sunset, stars or just clouds. Stay until it changes.

## Evenings at home

11. **Phone-free dinner.** Set the table properly, light a candle and stay for a while after the plates are empty.
12. **Board game night.** Winner picks dessert, the other one picks the next game.
13. **Cook a dish from a place you want to visit.** Talk about the trip while it simmers.
14. **Do a jigsaw puzzle.** Leave it out and come back to it all week.
15. **Build a blanket fort.** You are never too old. Bring snacks inside.
16. **Have a taste test.** Three kinds of chocolate, cheese or tea, tasted blind and ranked.
17. **Draw each other.** Five minutes, no peeking, reveal at the same time.
18. **Look through printed photos.** Tell the stories behind them, especially the ones from before you met.
19. **Bake something.** The kitchen will smell wonderful and you get to share the first warm piece.
20. **Candlelight hour.** Lights off, screens off, and see where the conversation goes.
21. **Write a story together.** Take turns adding one sentence. It will get ridiculous. That is the point.
22. **Make a time capsule.** Each write a letter to your future selves and choose a date to open it.
23. **Listen to a whole album.** Start to finish, lying on the floor, the way people used to.
24. **Give each other a shoulder rub.** Ten minutes each, with a timer in the kitchen rather than on a phone.

## Out of the house

25. **Take a long walk somewhere green.** A park, a trail or a river path.
26. **Go for a bike ride.** No route. Turn whenever one of you feels like it.
27. **Have a picnic.** A blanket and whatever is in the fridge is enough.
28. **Visit a museum or gallery.** Each choose the one piece you would take home.
29. **Wander a bookshop or library.** Pick a book for each other.
30. **Explore a neighborhood you rarely visit.** Walk until you find somewhere to eat.
31. **Go to a farmers' market.** Buy three things you have never cooked.
32. **Swim.** A pool, a lake or the sea. Phones do not like water anyway.
33. **Play something.** Bowling, table tennis, mini golf, frisbee in the park.
34. **Watch the sunrise.** Bring a flask of something warm and go back to bed afterwards.
35. **Stargaze.** Drive or walk a little way from the streetlights and lie on a blanket.
36. **Be tourists in your own town.** Do the thing visitors do that you never have.

## Dates that cost nothing

37. **Ask ten questions you have never asked.** "What did you want to be at nine?" is a good start.
38. **Dream out loud.** Talk about one thing you would love to do together in the next year.
39. **Tidy one corner as a team.** Music on. Oddly satisfying, and you both win.
40. **Teach each other something.** A recipe from your childhood, a knot, a card trick.
41. **Plant something.** A herb on the windowsill is enough. Decide who waters it.
42. **Retell how you met.** Each tell your own version. Compare the differences.

## When you have a whole day

43. **Take a day trip without a plan.** Choose a direction and stop when something looks good.
44. **Hike and eat lunch at the top.** The view is better when you are not looking at it through a screen.
45. **Have a slow day at home.** Long breakfast, a walk, a nap, cooking together, an early night. Nothing else.

## How to make it a habit

One good evening is nice. A rhythm is better. A few things help:

**Tie it to something that already happens.** Dinner, the walk after dinner, the last half hour before sleep. Habits stick more easily when they attach to something you already do every day.

**Start smaller than you think.** Twenty minutes a day that you actually keep beats a three-hour date night you cancel.

**Let the phone help you put it down.** Many couples find willpower alone does not last, especially in the evening when you are both tired. Pausing the most tempting apps during set hours takes the decision off your shoulders. On iPhone you can do this yourself with Screen Time, and we wrote a guide to [pausing social media at night on iPhone](/guides/pause-social-media-at-night-iphone/).

**Make it mutual.** A phone-free evening only works when you are both in. Agree on the hours together, keep the same rules for both of you, and change them together. Our guide to [screen time agreements for couples](/guides/screen-time-agreement-for-couples/) walks through that conversation.

**Celebrate it.** Notice out loud when an evening was good. "I liked tonight" is one of the most useful sentences in a relationship.
